# Game Vision: The Professor's Lost Laboratory

**Status:** The first two research chapters and a derived ending for the current 174-item laboratory are implemented and playable. The strongest reading of the Unsent Apology is that the Professor chose to follow the first stable impression by ordering an eastward survey toward the sealed bell chamber. Later records place that investigation before the bell and name the Professor and Elian as witnesses, but do not establish that his choice caused the Chaos Event. The Engine recorded an impression as a witness, not a cause; the Event's original trigger and Elian's response remain unresolved. Broader content and the full-game arc remain future work, and the independent human playthrough is still pending.

## Player promise

Turn the overwhelming clutter of an eccentric professor's steampunk laboratory
into an understandable archive, and use the relationships discovered while
sorting to reconstruct the professor's secret invention and the truth behind
the Chaos Event.

## Setting and tone

The laboratory was once a working archive and workshop. A still-unexplained
catastrophe scattered papers, instruments, components, and records across one
enormous connected space. The player arrives after the event and learns the
professor through the objects left behind: careful records, overconfident
annotations, eccentric letters, and experiments that do not always agree.

The tone combines mystery, mechanical wonder, and occasional dry humour. The
world should feel richly steampunk and arcane while keeping labels and controls
easy to read.

## Core loop

1. Scan a chaotic area for an object family, clue, or promising pattern.
2. Pick up items and compare them on temporary scratch pads.
3. Infer their category, sequence, or relationship from visual and written
   evidence.
4. File items into a final destination. Correct placement is confirmed; a
   wrong placement remains visible and marked for the player to recover.
5. Complete collections to earn a skill point and reveal related clues or
   puzzles.
6. Use organisational tools to spot relationships faster, then continue
   restoring the laboratory and the invention.

The player remains responsible for interpreting and sorting. Skills may reveal
information or highlight relationships, but do not finish the work
automatically.

## Objects and collections

Object families should be visually distinct and support more than one kind of
reasoning:

- **Documents:** blueprints, torn plans, equations, letters, journals, maps,
  field notes, experiment records, and coded messages.
- **Mechanical parts:** gears, keys, coils, labelled components, stamps, and
  prototype pieces.
- **Arcane records and specimens:** symbols, runes, formula cards, specimen
  cards, photographs, and strange artefacts.

Relationships can use labels, colour, shape, numbering, materials, subjects,
matching fragments, chronology, or compatibility. Important story and puzzle
items are authored; deterministic filler expands the world without obscuring
those relationships.

The eight-card **Harmonic Calibration** collection forms a glyph sequence that
opens the first pulse puzzle and points toward the professor's research.

The second research chapter adds three eight-item collections: **The Bell
Before Dawn**, **Meridian Survey**, and **Parallax Trials**. Each combines
handwritten notes, experiment records, map fragments, and formula cards. Their
dated clues gate **Chronology of the Last Bell**, which reveals a notebook
discovery and advances the Palimpsest Engine to its Chronology Wheel stage.

## Skills and discovery

The **Resonance Lens** highlights related objects and clue marks. The **Missing
Member Compass** highlights one loose member of a selected second-chapter
collection. Both help the player notice information without filing anything
automatically. The one-point passive **Modular Worktables** Workshop Skill adds
six temporary work spaces to each existing scratch pad, increasing capacity
from 24 to 30 without changing the boards or automating placement. The player
still interprets, arranges, and files every object.

The optional two-point **Aether Wayfinder** guides attention without doing the
filing: when activated for a selected item that is not correctly filed, it
focuses the item's canonical destination and highlights it for seven seconds.
This works for loose workspace items, scratch-pad items, and misfiled items;
the player still moves and files each item.

Later abilities may identify a likely destination, reveal a broad category,
highlight duplicates, or improve navigation. Story is delivered through
recovered records, relationships, collection reveals, puzzles, environmental
details, and the notebook; avoid long narrative interruptions.

## Secret invention

The selected concept is **The Palimpsest Engine**. It reads residual impressions
left on objects and reconstructs them as a luminous spatial replay. Its three
visible stages are a Dormant brass cradle with a dim, empty lens; a Memory
Crystal stage with a faceted, illuminated crystal; and a Chronology Wheel stage
that retains the crystal and adds a toothed, spoked wheel. When all
174 catalogued objects are correctly filed, every collection is complete, and
every catalogued puzzle is solved, the Engine presents an evidence-bounded
replay and derived completion statistics, then lets the player continue
exploring. It did not ring the bell; it replayed an impression left by the
Chaos Event. The Professor and Elian were both witnesses. The strongest reading
of the Unsent Apology is that the Professor chose to follow the first stable
impression by ordering an eastward survey toward the sealed bell chamber. Later
records place that investigation before the bell, but do not establish that his
choice caused the Event. The original trigger and Elian's response remain
unresolved.

Two alternatives were considered and not selected:

- **The Aether Loom** would connect related objects with glowing threads. Its
  visual payoff is strong, but it risks becoming only a visual version of the
  sorting mechanic.
- **The Chrono-Weaver** would model the events around the Chaos Event and
  project alternate outcomes. It offers spectacle, but time manipulation
  makes the mystery feel more familiar.

The Palimpsest Engine gives the ending a visual payoff tied to the object's
history while keeping the player's core task focused on organisation.

## Player experience goals

- The first view feels astonishingly cluttered but offers a few approachable
  patterns to start with.
- The player can form and test theories without committing them permanently.
- Errors are clear and recoverable, with no lives, timers, or arbitrary penalty.
- Completing a collection creates a noticeable local change and a meaningful
  new clue or tool.
- The laboratory remains one connected, persistent space; normal progress does
  not depend on unlocking separate levels or rooms.
- The notebook and progress indicators aid orientation without feeling like a
  spreadsheet.

The current fresh game starts with 174 entities. Expansion toward thousands
comes only after browser performance and playtesting support it.
